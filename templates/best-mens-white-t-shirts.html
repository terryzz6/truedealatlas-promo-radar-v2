These are picks based on construction and fit. Prices below are the US prices displayed during this check and may change.

What to Judge

Judge the collar and body separately: a straight-hanging shirt can still develop a stretched neckline. Judge shape and comfort separately after washing, too. And when replacing an old favorite, check the current garment’s fit and fabric rather than assuming an older version’s wear record applies. Weight identifies a fabric; it does not grade durability. Collar construction and the listed weights give you reasons to inspect these areas, but they cannot establish future wear.

What Repeated Washing Actually Changes

A 200g white tee was worn for a year and washed repeatedly. Its body did not noticeably lose shape, but its collar yellowed and became distorted. A separate 260g tee had a three-needle stitched collar that remained firm.

An older plain white tee from one shop held its shape for years. Later purchases from the same shop differed: a long-sleeved shirt shrank after washing, while a short-sleeved shirt felt harder afterward. In another account, a wearer’s original shirt size stopped fitting after their body changed.
